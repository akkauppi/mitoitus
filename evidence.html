TURNING

Wheelchair turning circles

SuRaKu: clear turning diameter at least 1.5 m for a wheelchair, 2.5 m for a powered wheelchair. The sheet draws them at a common scale. Keep the usable envelope clear (editorial application of clear-space guidance); do not infer that every mobility scooter fits this envelope.

Rescue geometry

TREES

Tree soil volume

Helsinki’s draft tree-and-tram card gives approximately 25 m³ conventional soil for a large-growing tree and discusses 40–60 m³ structural soil because stone displaces water storage. These are contextual indicative volumes, not a universal minimum. Coordinate roots, utilities and mature crown; grouped trees should have continuous soil.

SERVICES

Public services: three different population measures

Library: the 2010 ministry recommendation is about 100 m² useful floor area per 1,000 residents, with small municipalities often needing more per capita. The 10 × 10 m square visualises area only. Seinäjoki’s 2017 plan says population growth beyond 2,000 would bring a primary school and daycare; nearby schools were full. This is additional population and a local capacity trigger. Helsinki’s 2020 proposal assigns roughly 20,000 residents to an additional central-city health-station unit. It is a case population responsibility, not a minimum viable settlement. No universal service threshold is inferred; age structure, current capacity and catchment remain site inputs. TKK’s public-services section was unfinished; these are new sources.

BIKE-PARKING

Bicycle envelope and handling space

Helsinki typical cycle envelope is 0.6 m wide, 2 m long, 1.2 m high. Its rough one-row allowance is number of cycles × (0.6 m × 4 m), including handling space. Our plan shows five envelopes plus the remaining 2 m handling strip, not a specified rack design. Some racks need more length; special/cargo cycles require separate assessment. Espoo 811A keeps cycle/scooter parking out of the 5 m before specified crossings.

RAMPS

Ramp width, gradient and landings

SuRaKu: ramp minimum width 0.9 m, recommended minimum 1.2 m; recommended gradient ≤5%, maximum 8%. On long ramps steeper than 5%, straight intermediate landings at least 2 m every 6 m. The drawing is an illustrative 0.30 m rise at 5%: horizontal run = 0.30 / .05 = 6 m, followed by a 2 m landing. Both axes use the same scale. A 2 m landing drawn here does not imply that this particular short 5% ramp triggers the long-ramp rule. The landing includes a 1.75 m illustrative person at the same metric scale; body height/posture are drawing assumptions.

TRAM-CORRIDOR

Tram line corridors and lateral clearances

Espoo 2010 reserves at least 8 m for light rail, with stops separate. Helsinki’s draft street-space card gives at least 6 m for a line section. These are different source contexts. Regional guide: track centres on straight slab track at least 3 m; ballasted track minimum 3.1 m (3 m is approval-only). Normal separation from ATU edge to walking/cycling is 0.80 m at ≤40 km/h, 1.15 m at 50 km/h and 1.50 m above 50 km/h. Normal values are the general-planning starting point. Curves, poles, evacuation and crossings are additional design checks. Uncanted curve radii from the regional geometry table at 0.65 m/s² lateral acceleration: 48 m at 20 km/h, 107 m at 30 km/h, 190 m at 40 km/h. Transitions and clearance widening remain additional.

Tram stop cross-section

Regional tram guide: normal side-platform width 3.5 m; straight dedicated double-track width between platforms 5.5 m. TRAM-LENGTH

Tram stop length and levels

Regional guide: light-rail flat service area normally 45 m, 35 m by project agreement; Helsinki city-tram-only stop 30 m for one or 61 m for two. Add access ramps and crossing waiting areas. Ramp maximum 5%; platform longitudinal gradient normally ≤2%, crossfall 1–2%. Height above rail generally .30 m, Helsinki normally .27 m; select the vehicle/network context. The example assumes .30 m rise from adjacent level: .30/.05 = 6 m ramp at each end, hence 45 + 6 + 6 = 57 m before crossing waiting areas. The source’s approximate 5–5.5 m ramps depend on actual road/platform levels; the drawing uses its explicit assumption, not that approximate range. The stop plan includes two nominal 34 × 2.4 m tram-body silhouettes based on the regional guide’s five-part Artic XL table. Bodies and stop geometry share a scale. Joint/window details are illustrative; these are not the ATU clearance envelopes.

STEPS

Outdoor stair reference

SuRaKu 2008 card 3 gives minimum stair width 1.2 m, rise 0.12 m and recommended outdoor relationship 2 × rise + tread = .66 m. RAIN-GARDENS

Rain gardens and vegetated swales

Rain gardens combine temporary ponding, filtration, vegetation and, where conditions allow, infiltration. Swales convey water slowly; checks can increase storage. Soil, groundwater, contamination and maintenance need project-specific checks. These are design considerations, not additional course deliverables.

PONDS

Dry basins, permanent ponds and wetlands

Helsinki describes basins that empty between rains and basins with permanent water. Wetlands use shallow water and varied planted levels, typically tens of centimetres deep. Its indicative natural basin/wetland slopes are 1 vertical to 4–5 horizontal. The water-storage sections are conceptual. A separate dimensioned slope triangle shows 1 m vertical / 4 m horizontal = 25%; 1:5 means 20%. Live detention is the available volume above normal water level; full permanent water volume cannot be counted as empty storage. Outlet, overflow, banks and access must be included in the layout.

SOURCE-CONTROL

Green roofs, permeable paving, reuse and treatment chains

HSY lists green roofs, rain gardens and rainwater collection/reuse as runoff-reduction options, and surface or underground storage for gradual release. Permeable-pavement aggregate stores water in its voids; infiltration/drainage and clogging maintenance must be checked, including winter conditions. The roof and paving sections are conceptual, with no prescribed thickness or capacity. The matrix identifies design inputs rather than numerical requirements; roof wet load and storage availability require structural/hydrological design. A combined chain can reduce runoff, treat it, store it and discharge through controlled overflow.

PARKING-LAYOUT

Parking-bay and aisle dimensions

The 90° reference layout uses a 2.5 × 5 m interior bay and a 7 m two-way manoeuvring aisle. These values appear in TKK 2007 and Salo’s 2016 park-and-ride report; they are dated layout examples, not a claim that every present-day parking facility uses the same dimensions. TKK gives at least 2.75 m for an end bay. The repeating interior module does not include that end condition. Accessible parking is a separate layout: SuRaKu’s courtyard reference is at least 3.6 × 5 m, with at most 2% gradient in both directions; transfer, lift access and street-side layouts need their own checks. Vehicle bodies, if shown, use the separately cited reference silhouette dimensions and do not prove manoeuvrability.

PARKING-AREA

Bay area versus a repeating parking module

Geometric example derived from 2.5 m bay width, 5 m depth and a 7 m shared aisle: one bay is 12.5 m². Two facing bays plus their aisle strip occupy 2.5 × (5 + 7 + 5) = 42.5 m², or 21.25 m² per space. The aisle contributes 8.75 m² per space. Salo’s example gives 425 m² for 20 spaces using the same geometry. This is a repeating interior parking-and-aisle module, not the gross area of a complete site or building. Add wider end bays, entrances, pedestrian and accessible routes, landscape, drainage and snow storage; garages also need structures and ramps. No universal gross m²/space allowance is asserted.

PARKING-NORMS

Dated municipal residential parking minima

These are municipal apartment-building examples, not Finnish national ratios. Helsinki’s 9 Dec 2025 guidance gives at least one space per 250, 230 or 200 m² of statutory floor area in mapped zones 2, 3 or 4. In mapped general-plan centres C1/C2/C3, add 30 m² to the denominator. The zone map is essential; the zones are not simple station-distance rings. Fractions round upward. The city says a guidance minimum need not meet market demand. Espoo’s 2025 guide gives central-core apartment minima of one space per 130, 110 or 95 floor-m² for area classes I, II or III, typically 0–300 m from the station/core. Every apartment case also requires at least 0.5 space per dwelling; satisfy both constraints. At least 5% of the required spaces are visitor spaces. The source table has different values farther from the core, and separate conditional reductions. Check the applicable detailed plan, zone and project conditions; these reference examples do not replace them. They do not override the W4 exclusion of long-term private-car parking. The chart assumes 10,000 residential floor-m² and 100 dwellings, no permitted reductions, a Helsinki zone-3 location outside C1/C2/C3 centres and an Espoo class-I core. Helsinki: ceil(10,000/230)=44 spaces. Espoo: ceil(max(10,000/130,0.5×100))=77 spaces; visitor spaces are within this required total. Counts are minimum examples, not demand forecasts.

The original reference

The TKK / YKS page is dated 28 August 2007. Its HTML and content drawings have been preserved locally with URLs, retrieval metadata and SHA-256 checksums. The original includes building rules, parking layouts and recreation-area quantities; selected values are redrawn on the sheets with their 2007 date, never as current standards. Its light-traffic and public-service sections were unfinished. The new public-services panel uses separately identified ministry and municipal sources.

The new helper extends the source’s compact visual-reference approach with the W4 assignment’s municipal sources, accessible movement and downloadable reference geometry. Archived originals are separate from this public repository.

Source discrepancies retained

  • The Espoo stormwater page labels a link “2025–2034” while one target is the 2020 programme; another inspected target is a water-services development plan. The helper’s numerical claim comes only from the verified Kaavoitus passage on the page, not from a claimed 2025–2034 programme PDF.
  • The workbook’s Helsinki street-space PDF is dated May 2014. A growing-medium card refers to May 2015. They have not been treated as the same edition.
  • The Helsinki tram and tree/tram cards are marked draft by their publisher. Their values remain labelled with that context.
  • SuRaKu is the 2008 collection linked by the assignment. Its recommendations are not presented as a complete statement of current building regulations.
